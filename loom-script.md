# Loom script — Adaptive Skill Loop (≈55 seconds)

Calm, conversational pace (~140 words/min). The *Screen* lines say what to show while you speak.

---

**0–10 s · Design question**
*Screen: hero, with the loop diagram visible.*

> This is an independent learning-design concept inspired by Speexx Talk. It explores one question: what could happen when a language difficulty shows up not once, but repeatedly, across job-specific practice?

**10–25 s · Scenario and recurring observation**
*Screen: click "Use Anna’s sample response", then "Show simulated feedback". Hover over the 6 → 7 counter.*

> Anna is a QA engineer explaining a checkout bug to a developer. The feedback here is pre-scripted. What matters is how it's sorted: vocabulary and structure are in-the-moment tips, but the verb-form errors are a pattern she's shown before. That's now the seventh observation.

**25–40 s · Skill Profile and microlearning**
*Screen: "Update learning profile", then the profile and recommendation card, then answer two drill items quickly and show the gap-fill item (Q5).*

> The profile keeps that history, and the recurring skill becomes the priority. That triggers a two-minute practice that moves from spotting the form to writing it in a bug report. Finishing it doesn't erase the history. It only marks the skill as ready to recheck.

**40–55 s · Reassessment**
*Screen: "Try a new QA scenario", then "Use Anna’s improved response", then "Show simulated reassessment". Show the evidence highlights and the "Improvement observed / Skill mastered — not yet" cards.*

> Then the same skill is checked in a new situation: a file-upload bug. The verb forms are accurate, so the status becomes "Improving". It's not "fixed", because one success isn't stable evidence yet.

**55–60 s · Closing**
*Screen: the final loop row.*

> So, from isolated feedback to a continuous skill loop. Thanks for taking a look.
